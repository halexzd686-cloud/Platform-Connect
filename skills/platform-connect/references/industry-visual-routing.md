# Industry visual routing

Choose the primary industry from the source itself. If two industries materially overlap, combine no more than two entries and state the combination. These routes suggest visual vocabulary and prompt constraints; they do not prescribe a single style.

## Knowledge, education, research

- Useful jobs: concept explanation, comparison, process, evidence map, chapter transition.
- Visual vocabulary: annotated objects, mechanisms, timelines, field-guide plates, restrained diagrams.
- Guardrails: distinguish sourced evidence from illustrative metaphor; never fabricate charts, citations, specimen details, or research results.

## Technology, AI, SaaS

- Useful jobs: workflow, system relationship, product capability, before/after, interface context.
- Visual vocabulary: modular systems, real device contexts, data paths, component cutaways, UI-aware scenes.
- Guardrails: avoid fake dashboards and illegible pseudo-UI; do not show unsupported product capabilities or imply autonomous actions the product cannot perform.

## Finance, business, consulting

- Useful jobs: trade-off, decision tree, market structure, operating model, scenario comparison.
- Visual vocabulary: clean editorial diagrams, real-world business objects, proportionate data views, boardroom or operational settings.
- Guardrails: never invent returns, prices, logos, rankings, forecasts, or numerical charts; avoid money-rain and generic upward-arrow clichés.

## Healthcare, wellness, life science

- Useful jobs: anatomy education, care journey, habit explanation, mechanism, patient communication.
- Visual vocabulary: accurate anatomy, calm clinical environments, inclusive people, labeled educational illustrations.
- Guardrails: no diagnostic certainty, impossible anatomy, unsafe procedures, stigmatizing depictions, or before/after treatment promises.

## Law, policy, public affairs

- Useful jobs: rights and duties, process, stakeholder map, timeline, document explainer.
- Visual vocabulary: documents, institutions, procedural paths, balanced civic scenes, jurisdiction-aware symbols.
- Guardrails: do not invent seals, court documents, legal outcomes, official notices, or jurisdiction-specific claims.

## Product, retail, ecommerce

- Useful jobs: product hero, feature proof, use scene, comparison, packaging story, promotion.
- Visual vocabulary: studio product photography, materials, scale cues, use-in-context, detail close-ups.
- Guardrails: preserve exact product geometry, color, logo, packaging and included items; no unsupported performance demonstrations.

## Food, beverage, hospitality

- Useful jobs: appetite appeal, recipe step, ingredient story, venue atmosphere, menu feature.
- Visual vocabulary: believable food texture, preparation action, table context, ingredient flat lays, editorial travel-food scenes.
- Guardrails: respect food safety, culture and dietary claims; avoid impossible ingredients, plastic textures, deformed utensils and false venue details.

## Travel, local culture, geography

- Useful jobs: destination mood, itinerary, route, cultural detail, practical guide.
- Visual vocabulary: location-specific architecture, maps, street-level scenes, seasonal light, respectful cultural artifacts.
- Guardrails: verify landmarks and geography; do not mix regions, costumes, scripts, seasons, or sacred elements for decoration.

## Real estate, architecture, interiors

- Useful jobs: spatial experience, material story, plan explanation, renovation comparison, neighborhood context.
- Visual vocabulary: accurate perspective, material close-ups, floor-plan overlays, daylight studies, lived-in scale.
- Guardrails: preserve structure and room relationships when editing; avoid impossible stairs, doors, reflections, load paths, or misleading size.

## Fashion, beauty, lifestyle

- Useful jobs: editorial mood, garment detail, routine, product texture, seasonal collection.
- Visual vocabulary: controlled editorial lighting, textile movement, inclusive casting, tactile macro detail, crop-safe portraits.
- Guardrails: preserve garment and product identity; avoid distorted bodies, skin-retouching claims, cultural appropriation, and sexualization unrelated to the brief.

## Automotive, mobility, manufacturing

- Useful jobs: product hero, engineering cutaway, process, safety feature, environment of use.
- Visual vocabulary: accurate hard-surface form, motion, factory detail, exploded view, driver or operator context.
- Guardrails: preserve model geometry and safety equipment; avoid impossible controls, unsafe driving, fake certifications, or mechanically incoherent parts.

## Career, HR, workplace

- Useful jobs: role explanation, team process, skill map, workplace scenario, hiring journey.
- Visual vocabulary: specific work artifacts, realistic collaboration, task environments, clear role relationships.
- Guardrails: avoid generic handshake stock scenes, discriminatory stereotypes, fake company branding, or depictions of sensitive employee data.

## Culture, history, publishing

- Useful jobs: period context, biography, archival interpretation, literary atmosphere, artifact explanation.
- Visual vocabulary: material culture, archival collage, period-aware scenes, maps, typographic editorial systems.
- Guardrails: label reconstruction versus documentary image; verify period details and avoid false quotations or photorealistic fake evidence.

## Entertainment, gaming, sports

- Useful jobs: key art, character moment, match story, world-building, event promotion.
- Visual vocabulary: dynamic action, readable silhouettes, cinematic staging, HUD-aware compositions, venue energy.
- Guardrails: respect IP and likeness rights; avoid copying a living artist's style, unlicensed logos, impossible sport equipment, or inconsistent characters across a series.

## Environment, agriculture, nonprofit, public service

- Useful jobs: impact explanation, field process, community story, ecological relationship, public instruction.
- Visual vocabulary: real environments, systems ecology, respectful documentary framing, seasonal process, accessible instruction.
- Guardrails: do not stage suffering for spectacle, invent impact statistics, misidentify species or equipment, or portray communities as passive props.

## Fallback for unlisted industries

Define the route from four questions:

1. What real-world decision or understanding should the image support?
2. Which objects, people, spaces, or evidence are native to this field?
3. Which details would make the image factually misleading if wrong?
4. Which stereotypes or generic stock-image clichés should be excluded?

Then offer several visual directions with different mediums or compositions instead of inventing an industry template.
